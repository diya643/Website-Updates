The challenge

Results season began with days of recaps.

The research team covers 180 listed companies, and most of them report within the same few weeks each quarter. For every result, an analyst read the filing, the call transcript and the broker notes, checked the numbers against their own model and wrote a recap in the house template.

That took the first two to three days of every season. Fund managers waited, and the most valuable work, testing each thesis against the new numbers, started last.

Results

Every company, recapped on results day.

Analysts spend 60% less time on earnings processing. All 180 companies get a recap every quarter, about four hours after results are filed, and each analyst gets two to three days of the season back.

The team spends that time on the work recaps used to crowd out: testing theses, calling management and updating models.

Frequently asked questions

How does Pascal compare results with an analyst's own model?
Pascal reads the analyst's Excel model, maps its line items to the reported figures and shows the variance for each line. It also checks the thesis on file and marks each point as supported or contradicted.
Can Pascal write earnings recaps in our house template?
Yes. Pascal fills your Word template section by section, with your headings and order, and cites every figure to its page.
Which sources does the recap agent read for Indian companies?
Exchange results filings, investor presentations, earnings call transcripts, the broker notes your firm receives and your analysts' own models and notes.
Do analysts still review every recap?
Yes. Each recap goes to the covering analyst as a draft. The analyst edits and approves it before it reaches the fund managers.
What is the weekly signals agent?
A prototype that reads the week's broker notes, filings and news on coverage names and flags what touches each thesis. It runs with one sector team today.
