The challenge

The answers existed. In a hundred places.

Prospective clients send long RFPs and due diligence questionnaires on custody, fund accounting, transfer agency, information security and business continuity. The servicer had answered most of these questions before, in past responses, policy documents and control reports.

The RFP team searched old files, copied answers forward and chased product specialists to confirm what had changed. Reviewers corrected the same answers on every response.

  1. Answers in three kinds of filePast RFPs on shared drives, policies on SharePoint, control descriptions inside SOC 1 and ISAE 3402 reports.
  2. Copied forward by handEach response started from whichever old file someone remembered, with dates and figures to re-check.
  3. Edits that never stuckA reviewer's correction lived in one response. The next RFP started from the old wording again.

What Pascal did

From scattered files to a library that learns.

Pascal built the library once, then ran every new RFP through it. Reviewer decisions are stored in the context graph, so each response starts from the latest approved wording.

Frequently asked questions

How does Pascal build an approved answer library from past RFPs?
Pascal splits past RFPs, DDQs and policies into question-and-answer pairs, groups duplicates and links each answer to its source. Your compliance team approves the version that goes into the library.
How do reviewer edits improve future RFP answers?
Each edit is saved as a decision with the reason, the approver and where it applies. The library answer updates, so the next response starts from the corrected wording.
Can Pascal answer questions from SOC 1 and ISAE 3402 reports?
Yes. Pascal reads control descriptions in your assurance reports and cites the control and page, so reviewers can check the wording against the report.
Does Pascal keep the prospect's RFP template and numbering?
Yes. The response goes back in the prospect's own Excel or Word template with their numbering, and an internal copy keeps every source.
What happens when a question has no approved answer?
Pascal drafts it from your policies and product documents and marks it for a specialist. Once approved, the answer joins the library.
