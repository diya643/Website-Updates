The challenge

Three days to a first draft, mostly typing numbers.

Every pitch needed a comparable companies table. An analyst picked the peers, downloaded annual reports and quarterly results, typed each figure into the bank's Excel template, calendarised fiscal years and checked everything twice before a VP saw it.

A first-draft pitch book took about three days, and most of that went into spreading and checking. When a VP asked where a figure came from, the analyst went back to the PDF to find it.

Frequently asked questions

How does Pascal build a comparable companies table from filings?
Pascal reads each peer's annual report, quarterly results and investor presentation, extracts the line items your template needs and computes the multiples. Every cell keeps a link to the page it came from.
Can Pascal use our bank's Excel and PowerPoint templates?
Yes. Pascal fills your own comps workbook and places pages on your PowerPoint master, so the output matches the rest of the pitch book.
How does Pascal handle different fiscal year-ends?
Pascal calendarises each company to the year-end you choose, using quarterly results, and shows the calculation behind every adjusted figure.
How do reviewers check a number in the comps?
They click the cell. The filing opens at the source page with the figure highlighted, so a VP or MD can check any number in seconds.
Where does Pascal run at a bank?
This bank runs Pascal on premise inside its own network. Pascal also deploys in a private cloud, with access tied to deal-team permissions.
