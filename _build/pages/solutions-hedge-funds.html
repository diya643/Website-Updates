Frequently asked questions

How do hedge funds use AI across front, middle and back office?
In the front office, hedge funds use AI for event briefs, earnings recaps and thesis monitoring. In the middle office, it explains trade and reconciliation breaks and writes P&L and exposure commentary. In the back office, it drafts DDQ and RFP answers and investor letters. Pascal runs all three on one context graph, so each team reads from the same sources.
What does middle office mean at a hedge fund?
The middle office sits between the trading desk and fund administration. It covers trade capture and confirmation, reconciliations against prime brokers and custodians, P&L and exposure reporting, collateral and margin, and risk monitoring. The head of trading, the COO and, at larger funds, the CRO usually own it.
Does Pascal respect our broker research entitlements?
Yes. Pascal checks permissions before a document reaches an analyst or an agent. A report from a broker your fund doesn't license, or an internal note restricted to one team, stays hidden from everyone else, and the count of hidden documents is shown so nothing looks missing.
Which systems does Pascal connect to at a hedge fund?
Pascal reads broker research feeds, expert network transcripts, SharePoint and email, and the files your OMS, prime brokers and administrator already send. It also works with market data you license, such as Bloomberg, FactSet and Capital IQ.
How does Pascal keep fund data secure?
Pascal deploys in your private cloud or on premise, with role-based access controls and source-level permissions. Your positions, research and investor data stay inside your perimeter.
Where should a hedge fund start with Pascal?
Most funds start with one workflow on their own documents, usually event briefs or a broker research vault, then add the middle and back office on the same graph. See the workflow library for the full list.
